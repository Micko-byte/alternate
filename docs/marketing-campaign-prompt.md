# Prompt for Claude — the "ours vs the free AI" campaign

Paste everything below the line into a fresh Claude conversation, and attach the four files named in
**Assets**. Keep the file names the same so the references line up.

---

You are the art director for **ALTERNATE**, a virtual fitting room built in Nairobi. Kenyans shop on
Instagram, TikTok and at the stall, where you cannot try anything on before you send the M-Pesa.
ALTERNATE draws the piece onto a shopper's own photo, in their own size, and checks the result before
they see it. One try-on costs KES 25 (2 for KES 50). Stores list free and earn a cut of what their
followers spend.

I want a launch campaign built on one true, provable claim: **general AI image tools change the person.
Ours does not.**

## The proof I'm giving you

Same woman, same dress, two machines.

1. `her-photo.png` — her real photo. Plain wall, white tee, jeans, arms down, phone at chest height.
   Look at her bare upper right arm: **clean skin, no tattoo.**
2. `the-dress.jpg` — the actual piece: a mustard-and-white ditsy floral maxi with a thigh-high slit,
   photographed on a hanger.
3. `alternate-result.jpg` — what ALTERNATE produced. Her face, her hair, her pose, her body, wearing
   that print. Her arm is still clean.
4. `chatgpt-result.png` — what ChatGPT produced from the same two inputs. It is a good-looking picture
   and it is wrong: **it drew a tattoo on her upper right arm that does not exist**, and it redrew the
   garment instead of keeping the real one.
5. `chatgpt-session.mp4` — an 82-second screen recording of that ChatGPT session, for the video cut.

Check the arm crops yourself before you design anything, and build the campaign on what you actually
see. If you find other differences — the print repeat, the strap, the slit, the neckline, the body
shape, the shadows — use those too. Do not invent a difference that is not in the files.

## The idea to land

A tattoo she does not have is a small mistake that says everything: if a tool will invent ink on her
skin, it will invent how the dress fits. The campaign should feel like evidence, not advertising —
closer to a lab notice or a receipt than to a glossy fashion ad. Confident, dry, a little funny. Never
sneer at the shopper for using free tools, and never name-call the other product; show its output and
let it speak.

Kenyan English, the way people actually write — no imported American slang, no forced Sheng.

## Brand

Ink `#191710` · Paper `#F3F1EC` · Navy `#222A41` · Mustard `#DBAE49` · Muted `#686868` · Rule `#E4E2DC`.
A heavy geometric display face for headlines, a mono face in small caps with wide letter-spacing for
labels and kickers, plain sans for body. Hard square corners, hairline rules, generous white space,
one mustard accent per composition. Photography is real people in real rooms — nothing looks like
stock. Dark mode is ink ground, paper type.

## What to make

1. **The campaign line** — three options, one recommended, each under seven words.
2. **The hero comparison.** One image, phone-shaped, that puts ALTERNATE's result beside ChatGPT's with
   the invented tattoo circled and labelled. It must read at thumbnail size in a feed.
3. **Three social posts** (1080×1350) that each carry one idea: the tattoo, the dress the machine
   redrew, and the price — with captions of 20–40 words and a first line that works as the preview.
4. **A 15-second vertical video cut** as a shot list with on-screen text and timings, using the screen
   recording as the "what the free tool did" section and the real photos as the payoff.
5. **A landing-page section** for alternate-two.vercel.app that carries the same comparison, in the
   brand above, responsive from 375px, with the honest caption that says which image came from where.
6. **One line of legal-safe wording** for the comparison, since we are showing another company's output.

For each deliverable say what it is for and where it runs. Build the hero image and the landing section
as real artifacts I can look at, not descriptions.

## Rules

- Every number must come from this brief: KES 25 a try-on, 2 for KES 50, M-Pesa, Nairobi.
- No invented testimonials, no fake follower counts, no made-up accuracy percentages.
- Say "her photo", not "your data". Say what the product does, not what the AI is.
- The woman in these photos is a real person: nothing that mocks her body, and no claim she endorses us.
