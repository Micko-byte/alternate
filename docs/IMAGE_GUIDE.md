# VAA ALTERNATE image guide

Every photo on the public site is served from Cloudinary (cloud `dnrj0hbpy`), folder `ALTERNATE/site/<slot>` for the landing page
and `ALTERNATE/onboarding/1-4` for the phone welcome slides. Upload a new image with the same public ID (overwrite on) to replace one.
Cloudinary picks the format and sends phones a smaller copy. If an image is missing, the page shows a designed placeholder.

## The look (applies to every photo)

- **Real, not stock.** Real people from Nairobi wearing real pieces from partner stores. Stock photos make a try-on app look fake.
- **One light.** Soft daylight (open shade, big window) or one large softbox. Warm-neutral white balance. Expose for skin, so darker skin tones keep their detail.
- **Calm backgrounds.** Off-white walls, concrete, or a softly blurred Nairobi location. Let the clothes carry the colour.
- **One grade.** Edit every photo with the same preset so the site feels like one shoot.
- **Clothes are the subject.** Faces can be cropped or turned away in the shop callouts; the garment must be sharp.
- **Different bodies.** Women and men, sizes 8 to 20, different heights. Shoppers need to see someone like them.
- **Proof, not promises.** Any "result" image must be a real VAA ALTERNATE try-on (Studio quality), not a retouched photo.
- **Permission.** A signed model release for every person, and written OK from every store whose piece, rail or post appears.

**File rules:** JPG, sRGB, quality around 80, longest side 2400 px or less, ideally under 500 KB (most visitors are on mobile data).
Replacing a photo later? Use a new file name or ask for the cache to be bumped, because installed apps keep images.

## Getting the photos

A half-day shoot covers everything: one photographer, two or three models (at least one woman, one man, and different body sizes), and 8 to 10 pieces lent by two or three partner stores.
Shoot the customer-style photos first (front, side, arms visible), run them through VAA ALTERNATE for the result images, then shoot the editorial callouts. The stores keep the photos of their pieces, which makes it an easy trade.
